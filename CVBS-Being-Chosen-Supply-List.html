Conference Venues and Booking Services

Ten minutes from you finishes a job we cannot finish for you.

The website now tells an AI assistant exactly who CVBS is. What it cannot do is prove it. Everything below is something only CVBS holds, and each one is the difference between an assistant naming CVBS and an assistant hedging.

Why this matters, in one paragraph. Three in four event planners now use an AI assistant somewhere in their venue search. When an organiser asks one of them who should source a venue, the assistant does not read the CVBS website and decide. It assembles an answer from records it did not write: LinkedIn, Google listings, business registers, industry directories. It then checks the website to see whether the answer holds up.

The website side is now built. Every page carries a single machine-readable record of CVBS: the four people, the services, the areas covered, the fact it costs the organiser nothing, and the year it started. What is missing is anything that ties that record to a source outside the website. At the moment CVBS looks, to a machine, like a business with no independent existence.

The three that matter most

1
Highest impact

The links that prove CVBS exists

Whichever of these CVBS has, we need the exact web address. If one does not exist, say so and we will treat it as a separate decision rather than chase it.

  • The CVBS LinkedIn company page
  • The Google business listing, the one that appears in Maps
  • Facebook and Instagram, if they are the business accounts rather than personal

Nothing on the site currently connects CVBS to a single record anywhere else. This one gap does more damage than everything else on this page combined, and it takes about two minutes to close.

Send: copy the address out of your browser bar on each page and paste them into a reply.
2
Highest impact

The ABN, and the exact registered name

The eleven digit ABN, and the legal entity name exactly as it is registered, which may not be the trading name.

CVBS says “since 1989” sixteen times across the website and there is currently nothing anywhere that backs it up. Longevity is the strongest single argument CVBS has, and right now it is a claim rather than a fact. The business register turns it into a fact that can be checked, which is exactly what an assistant does before it puts a name in front of someone.

Send: the ABN. We will find and link the register entry ourselves.
3
Highest impact

Two clients who will put their name to a sentence

Two named people, at named organisations, who are happy to be quoted with their real name, role and employer. A short written approval by email is enough.

The two quotes on the site at the moment are from “Sarah M.” and “David R.” Nobody can repeat those, which means they do no work at all when someone is deciding whether to recommend CVBS. One named quote from a recognisable organisation is worth more than twenty anonymous ones, and it is the piece competitors will find hardest to match.

Send: name, role, organisation, the quote, and their written OK.

Worth doing at the same time

4
Quick fix

The client logo wall needs a look

Three of the ten logos on the homepage are for brands that no longer trade under that name:

  • Australand was acquired in 2014 and has traded as Frasers Property Australia since 2015
  • BT Financial Group was wound down into Westpac
  • Heritage Bank merged in 2023 and now trades as People First Bank

These are real relationships and worth keeping if CVBS is happy to. The question is whether to show the old logo, the current one, or drop it. A 2026 page showing three brands that no longer exist reads as a business that has not worked in a while, which is the opposite of what the wall is there to do.

Decide: keep as is, update to the current brand, or remove. And confirm we can name all of them in writing.
5
Quick fix

Memberships, accreditations and insurance

Anything CVBS belongs to or holds. Meetings and Events Australia, a chamber, a bureau partnership, professional indemnity, anything with a certificate.

For a service business with nothing physical to inspect, membership of an industry body is one of the shortcuts used to judge whether a business is real and current. If CVBS holds none, that is a genuine question worth discussing, because joining one is cheap and it works.

Send: the body, the membership number if there is one, and the year joined.
6
Quick fix

Confirm the business address

We have found 43A Flinders Road, Cronulla NSW 2230 in the email templates, and a PO Box 303 elsewhere. Which is the correct business address to publish?

A verifiable street address is a small but real legitimacy signal, and it also links the website to the Google listing in point one.

Confirm: which address, and whether CVBS is happy for it to be public.
7
When you can

Chantelle and Rychelle, in full

Surnames, confirmed job titles, and direct email addresses if they should be listed.

Karen and Anthony are now named in the site’s underlying data as real, identifiable people with roles. Chantelle and Rychelle appear on the page but only as first names, so they carry the warmth and none of the credibility. Four named people is one of the few things a venue marketplace genuinely cannot copy, and it is worth having complete.

Send: full names, titles, and whether to publish their emails.
8
A decision, not a task

Day delegate package ranges

Whether CVBS is willing to publish indicative day delegate package ranges by city and venue tier.

We checked the whole market. Capacity data is published widely. Rates are published partly. Nobody in Australia publishes day delegate package ranges, which means every organiser trying to build a budget has nowhere to go. Whoever publishes them first becomes the source that answer comes from, and the reason an assistant gives for recommending CVBS then writes itself: they publish the pricing the rest of the market will not.

This is the single biggest opportunity on the table and also the one that needs the most thought, because it means giving away information CVBS currently holds. Worth a proper conversation rather than a yes or no.

Discuss: is CVBS willing, and if so with what caveats.

What happens once these come back

Points one, two and six go straight into the site and take under an hour. Point three needs a short email from you to two clients. Points four, five and seven are decisions rather than work.

From there the measure is simple, and we run it monthly: put the questions an organiser would actually ask to ChatGPT, Gemini and Google’s own answers, write down what comes back, and compare it with last month. The test is not where CVBS ranks. It is whether CVBS gets named, and whether the reason given is true.
