1The role

A department, not a task list.

CVBS has never had a digital function. It has had digital tasks, done well, inside a social media retainer. This defines the function properly, so it is clear what CVBS is buying and who is accountable when the number moves.

Authority
To recommend and then implement digital activity without line by line approval. Four things stay the Directors' call: money committed on CVBS's behalf, the commission and pricing position, anything published in Karen's or Anthony's name, and any change to the brand.

What the role owns

  • Digital strategy. Where the enquiries come from over the next twelve months, and what gets built to make that happen.
  • The website. Offers, venue and destination content, the venue index, page performance, technical health, and the copy standard.
  • Social, three channels. Instagram and Facebook continue as they are. Karen's LinkedIn is written from what Karen already knows and published in her name. The company page carries clients, results, the team and each new index city.
  • Email. The program, the templates, list growth and hygiene, sending through Mailchimp, Act! kept as the record rather than the sender.
  • Content. Venue and destination features, offer write ups, blog and resource pages, carousels, photography direction, the image library.
  • Measurement. Analytics and Search Console, the monthly report, the quarterly enquiry review, and the check on whether AI assistants name CVBS when an organiser asks who should source their venue.

Instagram and Facebook are already running and already have an audience built over years. Nothing here is a repair of that. Five of these six are what is new.

2The rhythm

What happens each week.

Two and a half days of capacity a week, spread across four days so the channels are never dark for long.

DayWhat gets done
MondayWeek planned. Social written and scheduled across all three channels. Karen's LinkedIn post drafted for approval.
TuesdayWebsite. Offers loaded and expired ones removed, venue and destination content published, index rows added, technical checks.
WednesdayEmail built, proofed and sent. In non-send weeks this becomes long form content: features, resources, blog.
ThursdayCarousels and image work. Site visit material processed. Enquiries checked with Karen. Numbers read, next week set.
FridayNot committed. Available to CVBS as separately quoted work when something genuinely cannot wait.

And beyond the week

  • Every month. One page performance report: traffic and source, enquiries, email, social, and what it means for next month. Two emails sent. One venue or destination feature published. The assistant check run.
  • Every quarter. Enquiry review with Karen and Anthony. One new city added to the venue index. Listings and business details checked against one source of truth.
  • Every year. The digital plan for the next twelve months, with the budget it needs and the things CVBS should stop doing.
The one thing that breaks this

Content waiting on approval. Anything submitted is treated as approved after three business days so the week does not stall. Later approvals are honoured, they just move to the following week.

3The boundary

What is in, and what is not.

The website is the line that matters, because without it a monthly fee can quietly be asked to carry a second build.

Inside the retainerA separate project, quoted first
Creating, editing and maintaining pagesA redesign, a rebuild, or a new site
Offers, venue and destination content, the indexNew custom functionality of any kind
Blog, resource and landing pagesThird party integrations and APIs
Copy, images, internal links, metadataBooking, payment or e-commerce systems
Search, assistant and conversion improvementsMember logins, portals or gated areas
Technical health, uptime, broken links, speedMigration to a different platform or host

The test: work on the site as it exists is inside the retainer. Work that makes the site do something it could not do before is a project, priced before it starts.

Also outside the fee

  • Quoted separately. The new website build. Brand identity work. Video production. Professional photography and the photographer's fee.
  • Charged at cost, approved first. Advertising spend. Mailchimp, hosting, domains and software. Stock imagery. Travel to events or site visits.
  • Not the role. Answering venue enquiries, quoting clients, negotiating with venues or managing bookings. The role brings the enquiry to CVBS. CVBS converts it.
4What CVBS supplies

The other half of it working.

Most digital retainers underperform for one reason. The agency is waiting on the client and nobody wrote it down.

  • Karen's judgement, monthly. Thirty minutes on the phone or a voice note. What a room is actually like, what worked, what she would not do again. Nothing replaces it and nothing can be invented in its place.
  • Real photography. Venue and event images CVBS has the right to use. Stock cannot carry a venue finding business.
  • Approvals within three business days.
  • Enquiry outcomes, quarterly. Quoted, converted, value. Without it the review measures traffic instead of revenue.
  • Commercial facts, confirmed once. The commission rate as it is actually charged, the correct team titles, and what venue rate information may be published.
  • Owner level access to the domain, hosting, analytics, Search Console, Mailchimp, Act! and all social accounts, held in CVBS's name.

Where one of these is not met, the affected deliverable moves and is noted in that month's report rather than absorbed silently.
