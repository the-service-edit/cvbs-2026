Why $48,000 for the website

Three independent methods land in the same place. The published Australian tier that matches this build by page count and scope is Butterfly's $25,000 to $50,000 band, described as "15 to 50 pages, custom design and UX, standard integrations, WCAG compliance". This site is 52 published pages with a bespoke structured data product on top, which pushes it against the ceiling of that tier and into the edge of the next one ($50,000 to $100,000). Airtasker's aggregated Australian data puts agency custom design for larger and more complex sites at $40,000 to $75,000. And a disciplined market delivery of this scope is roughly 460 hours, which at $48,000 works out at $104 an hour, almost exactly Cemoh's published 2026 Australian average of $107 an hour for a web developer and $108 for a digital marketing strategist. You would be charging one average discipline rate for four disciplines delivered.

Why $8,650 a month for the ongoing role

Bought as separate services at published Australian rates, this scope costs CVBS between $6,500 and $12,000 a month, with the honest midpoint near $9,000. As a fraction of a salary, a Sydney Digital Marketing Manager sits at $115,000 to $135,000 market (Big Wave Digital, 4 August 2026); at half time, plus 12 per cent super and the standard contractor loading for leave, insurance, tools and downtime, that is $8,500 to $9,450 a month. As a day rate, $8,650 across 10.8 reserved days is $801 a day against Cemoh's Australian averages of $863 for a developer and $867 for a strategist. Three methods, three answers, all within a few hundred dollars of each other. The number you already had is correct and I am not moving it.

The one thing that could sink this, and it is not either number

Year one lands at roughly $145,000 against the $26,000 CVBS pays you today. Neither figure is wrong. The total is the problem, and it only becomes a problem if the document lets them add it up. Do not print an annual total, do not print a year one total, and do not put the build fee and the monthly fee in the same visual block. Phase one is a project with a start and an end. Phase two is what the business costs to run. They are different purchases and the document has to make them feel that way. Section 22 covers the rest of the risk.

The finding you will not enjoy, and it is the most useful one here

At 674 realistic hours, $48,000 is $71 an hour. That is below the Australian freelance average and well below the rate CVBS already pays you through the social retainer. Your hours are real. The problem is that they exceed what this asset is worth on the open market, because a large amount of the work was done before anyone committed to buying it. The August audit already recorded this: roughly $61,000 of sum of parts value produced unpaid for a client who was already paying you.

Do not try to recover the overbuild in the price. CVBS will not pay for your process. Price the asset at market, take the cash, and change the process. A disciplined delivery of this same site is 420 to 500 hours, and at $48,000 that is $96 to $114 an hour, which is where this should sit. The rule that stops this happening again

Before any future client approves a direction: build the design system, the homepage, two templates and one flagship page. Nothing else. Password or robots block the preview, never hand over a root URL, and charge a concept fee of $2,500 to $4,500 credited against the build on approval. You would have $4,500 in hand right now and 300 fewer unpaid hours behind you.

06Work already done

Valuing the work already completed

You asked me not to value the website on the remaining development hours. That is right, and here is how to make the argument without ever saying "I have already done a lot of work", which invites the reply "so it should cost less".

Three arguments, in order of strength

1. The fee buys the asset, not the calendar

CVBS is not buying twelve weeks of your time. They are buying a fifty two page website with a verified venue data product, an entity layer and a conversion architecture, that they will own outright and that has no dependencies to renew. A quote is a price for a finished thing. Nobody itemises a builder's hours after the house is standing. Never present the fee as a rate multiplied by a duration, because the moment you do, the negotiation becomes about duration.

2. The risk has already been taken, and that has value

An agency quoting this project today would price in discovery risk, direction risk, revision risk and the possibility that the client rejects the concept. Those contingencies are real money and they are usually 15 to 25 per cent of a fee. CVBS is not paying them, because the concept exists, they have seen it, and they approved it. They are buying a de risked project at the price of a risky one. That is the single best value line available to you and it is entirely true.

3. Continuity is a discount they are already receiving

Any other supplier starts with discovery: learning the business, the audience, the commission model, the venue relationships, the 37 year history, the reason CVBS will not publish rates. Butterfly, Luminary and every agency in the research price that as its own phase. You have done it over twelve months of running their social. That is 40 to 55 hours of discovery CVBS does not have to fund, plus the months of elapsed time it would take.

What this means for the fee

The strategy, competitor work, information architecture, conversion planning, visual direction, page concepts, UX decisions, design development and revisions are phases 01 to 04 of the project, worth 145 to 195 hours in the model above, roughly 28 per cent of the total. They are delivered work, not sunk cost. They belong in the scope of work, priced, with the rest of the phases, and the fee is invoiced against milestones that include them.

The practical mechanism

Make the first milestone 40 per cent on acceptance rather than a deposit. A deposit is money against work not yet done. Forty per cent on acceptance is payment for four phases that are already delivered and that CVBS is approving by signing. That single word change is worth $19,200 in your bank account in the first fortnight, and it is honest.

Why not the two structures you named

50 on commencement, 50 before launch leaves half the fee riding on a launch date CVBS controls. They owe you photography, the confirmed commission rate, Chantelle and Rychelle's titles, the decision on publishing venue pricing and approvals within three days. If any of those slip, and one of them will, half the fee sits unpaid while you wait. Never let a payment milestone depend on a client obligation.

40 commencement, 30 design approval, 30 before launch has a milestone that has already happened. The design is approved; that is why you are writing this document. Billing for a milestone in the past looks like padding.

The clauses that do the work

  • Revisions. Two rounds per template at design stage, one round per page at content stage. Beyond that, quoted before it starts. Say "rounds", not "changes", so a batch of notes is one round rather than eleven.
  • Definition of scope change. Anything that adds a page type, adds a function the site cannot currently perform, changes an approved template after sign off, or requires a third party system. New content in an existing template is not a scope change; a new kind of page is.
  • How extra work is charged. Fixed price, quoted and agreed in writing before it starts. No hourly rate anywhere in the client facing document, including here. If they push for an hourly figure for small jobs, the answer is a minimum half day priced as a fixed fee.
  • Hosting until final payment. The site stays on your hosting until the final invoice clears, then transfers. Standard, and it is the only real leverage in a project like this.
  • Client obligations with dates. Photography, the commission rate, approvals within three business days, account access at owner level in CVBS's name. Write them into the project scope with dates, not just the retainer.
  • A defined end. The project ends 30 days after launch. That date is what turns everything after it into retainer work rather than a build that never closes.

If cash flow is raised

Split the 40 per cent into two monthly instalments of $9,600. Do not move to twelve equal monthly payments, because a project invoiced like a subscription starts to feel like one, and it will make Phase two harder to price.

Recommendation: two planned campaigns a month

It is the right number for the list, it matches the position already documented, and it is what the recommended fee is built on. It is also what the eleven email program was designed around. Twenty four sends a year is a real email program for a business this size.

Urgent and ad hoc sends

The wording that protects you without sounding defensive:

"Two planned campaigns a month, agreed in the calendar. If something comes up that has to go out at short notice, we will swap it for one of that month's planned sends. If both are already out the door, I will quote it before I start."

Swap, do not stack. That single mechanic is what stops a two a month program becoming a five a month one over six months without anybody deciding to.

What the coordination is worth

This is the part to make concrete, because "cross channel coordination" on its own is agency language and it does not sell.

  • No brief written twice. The offer is written once and adapted into a page, a send and a carousel by the person who wrote it. A separate email agency would need a brief, a call, two rounds and a week.
  • No handover gap. The email lands on a page that was updated before it went out, not after, because the same person does both in the same week.
  • One message. The site, the send and the post say the same thing in the same words, which is what makes a position stick rather than a campaign happen.
  • Speed. An offer can be live on the site, in an email and on social within two days. Three suppliers cannot do that at any price.
  • No relearning. Every new supplier has to be taught the commission model, the venue relationships, why CVBS will not publish rates and what Karen will and will not say. That cost is real and CVBS pays it once per supplier, per year.

17Cadence

The recommended retainer, and how to bill it

While the site is built
$6,900 per month

From signing. Social across three channels, email, strategy and coordination begin immediately. The build is invoiced separately against its own milestones.

From launch day
$8,650 per month

The full Head of Digital scope, including weekly management of the new site.

If they ask
$3,992 per fortnight

Arithmetically identical. I would not offer it.

Bill monthly. This is not a small decision.

You currently invoice $500 a week. The instinct is that fortnightly keeps continuity and softens the number. It does the opposite.

  • A weekly or fortnightly figure is divisible and a monthly one is not. $2,000 a week is four times $500 a week, and everyone in the room can do that in their head in a second. $8,650 a month is a role, and a role has to be compared to other roles, which is a comparison you win.
  • Frequency signals size. Things billed weekly are services. Things billed monthly are functions. You are asking to be treated as a function.
  • Cash flow is better, not worse. Monthly in advance with fourteen day terms means you hold a month of money. Fortnightly in arrears means you are always half a month behind.
  • Fewer invoices, fewer approval events. Twelve moments a year where somebody looks at the number, rather than twenty six.

The transition line to use, once: "the social invoicing moves from weekly to monthly, in line with how the whole engagement is now structured". Say it once, in the terms, and never again.

Separate the cadence from the scope in the document

The scope is a monthly retainer covering the six functions. The billing is monthly in advance, fourteen days, plus GST. Those are two different sentences in two different places, and keeping them apart is what stops the conversation becoming about the invoice instead of the job.

22Critique

Risks, and an honest critique of this proposal

The weakest part

There is no evidence in this proposal that the last twelve months of social produced anything. CVBS has paid $26,000 for it. You are about to ask them to roughly quadruple that and add a $48,000 project on top. The first question a sensible director asks is what the existing spend has returned, and right now the answer is not written down anywhere. Before you present, put one page together showing what the social has done: audience growth, the site visits and venue content it produced, enquiries or conversations it can be linked to, anything at all with a number on it. If you cannot produce that page, the retainer increase is being argued entirely on scope and inputs, and inputs are the weakest thing to sell on.

What feels generic

"Cross channel coordination" and "digital strategy" are the two weakest lines in the whole structure. Every agency in the research says both. Section 12 has the concrete version and it should replace the abstract one everywhere it appears: one brief written once, the page updated before the email goes out, an offer live across three channels in two days, and nobody relearning the commission model. Specifics survive scrutiny; category names do not.

What a competitor would do

A boutique studio pitching against you would do three things. They would quote the build lower and the retainer lower, and make it up in scope creep, because they can afford a loss leader on a first project. They would offer a CMS so CVBS can "make their own changes", which sounds like independence and is really a way of making the site cheaper to build and the client feel in control. And they would offer paid advertising, because it is the easiest place to add margin and it makes the reporting look busy. You should have an answer ready to all three, and the answers are: the price is the scope; the site has zero dependencies and nothing to renew, which is worth more to a business that kept the last one fourteen years; and paid advertising is available but it is not what is broken, so it is quoted separately when it is the right move.

What would make this impossible to ignore

One thing, and it is not in the document yet. Translate the fee into bookings using CVBS's own budget calculator. It returns $44,800 to $72,100 for a 120 delegate two day capital city conference. The commissionable portion is $39,600 to $62,400, and at the standard published rate that is $4,000 to $6,200 of commission per conference. So the entire website is eight to twelve mid size conferences, once, and the monthly retainer is roughly one and a half conferences a month. That converts an expense into a volume target, in their unit, using their calculator. Confirm the actual commission rate with Karen first, because the whole argument fails if the rate is wrong.

The four risks, ranked

  1. Cannibalisation, not rejection. The likely bad outcome is not "no". It is "let's pause the social spend for a year to fund the website". Name it before they do: the site is what makes the existing content spend pay, so switching one off to fund the other leaves them with a new website nobody is driving traffic to.
  2. The total. Roughly $145,000 in year one against $26,000 today. Never show an annual figure, never sum the two phases, and keep them in visually separate parts of the document.
  3. Concentration. CVBS becomes 50 to 70 per cent of your revenue. Keep the 60 day notice, keep the IP split, keep the hosting clause, and do not let the rest of your client base go quiet while this is happening.
  4. The precedent. Roughly 674 hours have gone into this before a fee was agreed. CVBS's reasonable expectation, formed over a year, is that work appears. The scope boundaries in sections 18 and 19 are not administrative detail, they are the correction to that expectation, and they only work if you enforce them in the first month.

Two things to confirm before you present

  • The commission rate, as CVBS actually charges it. The bookings argument depends on it entirely and it is still unverified.
  • How many hours a week the social actually takes you. Not for the document, for you. If $500 a week has been buying twelve or fifteen hours, the whole engagement has been underpriced for a year and the new number is a correction rather than an increase, which changes how you feel walking into the room.
