1What this is

A department, not a task list.

CVBS has never had a digital function. It has had digital tasks, done well, by one person, inside a social media retainer.

Primary purpose
To own, run and continuously improve the whole of CVBS's digital presence: the website, the social channels, email, content, search and assistant visibility, and the measurement behind all of it. The role carries both the strategy and the execution.
Authority
To recommend and then implement digital activity within brand, commercial and budget parameters agreed with the Directors. Anything that commits CVBS money, changes the commercial position, or contradicts an instruction from the Directors is proposed, not enacted.

The current arrangement buys social content. Everything else that has happened around it, the website rebuild, the venue index, the email program, the positioning work, the assistant monitoring, has been produced outside the scope CVBS is paying for.

That was the right way to prove the case. It is not a way to run a business channel. This document defines the function properly so that CVBS knows what it owns, what it is buying, and who is accountable when the number moves.

The title matters less than the accountability. Head of Digital is used here because the role carries the strategy, not only the output. If CVBS would prefer the role to execute a plan set elsewhere, the correct title is Digital Marketing Manager and the scope in Part 8 changes with it.

2The six functions

What the role owns.

Each of these is a job somebody has to do. At the moment none of them has a name against it.

  • 1Digital strategyWhere the enquiries are going to come from over the next twelve months, and what gets built to make that happen. Positioning, channel priority, the annual plan, and the call on what CVBS does not do.
  • 2The websiteOwnership of the live site once it launches. New and expiring offers, venue and destination content, the venue index, page performance, technical health, and the copy standard the whole site is written to. What counts as ongoing website work, and what counts as a new project, is defined in Part 5.
  • 3Social, three channelsInstagram and Facebook continue as they are, now aligned to the site's design language. Comments and direct messages are monitored on business days and answered within one business day, or passed to Karen where they are a live enquiry. Karen's LinkedIn is ghost written from what Karen already knows and published in her name. The CVBS company page carries the company layer: clients, results, the team, each new city of the index.
  • 4EmailThe twelve email program, template production, list growth and hygiene, sending through Mailchimp, and keeping Act! as the record rather than the sender. Offers checked against expiry before every send.
  • 5Content productionEverything above needs words and pictures. Venue and destination features, offer write ups, blog and resource pages, carousels, photography direction from site visits, and the image library that feeds all of it.
  • 6Measurement and visibilityAnalytics and Search Console, the monthly performance report, the quarterly enquiry review, and the assistant check that tracks whether CVBS is named when an organiser asks an AI assistant who should source their venue.
Worth saying plainly

Instagram and Facebook are already running and already have an audience built over years. Nothing in this document is a repair of that work. Functions two, four, five and six are the ones that are genuinely new.

The one thing that breaks this

Content waiting on approval. Anything submitted for approval is treated as approved after three business days so the week does not stall. Approvals that arrive later are honoured, they just move to the following week.

4The month, the quarter, the year

The longer rhythm.

Weekly work keeps the channels alive. This is the work that compounds.

Every monthPerformance report. One page. Traffic and source, enquiries received, email sent and read, social reach and engagement, and what it means for next month. Not a dashboard screenshot.
Assistant check. Forty questions across four AI surfaces, first Tuesday, same wording every month. Two numbers reported: how often CVBS is named, and how often the reason given is accurate. The wrong or missing claims become the next month's content plan.

Every quarterEnquiry review with Karen and Anthony. What came in, what converted, what the enquiries had in common, and what that changes. This is the meeting the whole retainer is judged on.
One new city added to the venue index, researched, written and published. Four cities a year.
Entity and listing refresh. Every place CVBS is described online checked against one source of truth so the facts do not drift.
Every yearDigital plan for the following twelve months, presented, with the budget it needs and the things CVBS should stop doing.

The assistant check is deliberately a monitoring exercise, not a promise. These systems are not deterministic. Nobody can guarantee CVBS will be named, and one month's movement is weather rather than a trend. Three months is a trend. That limit is stated here so it is never implied elsewhere.

5The boundary

What is not included.

A scope is only useful if it has an edge. These sit outside the retainer and are quoted separately, in writing, before anything starts.

Where the website line sits

This is the boundary that matters most, because without it a weekly fee can quietly be asked to carry a second build.

Inside the retainerA separate project, quoted first
Creating, editing and maintaining pagesA redesign, a rebuild, or a new site
Offers, venue and destination content, the indexNew custom functionality of any kind
Blog, resource and landing pagesThird party integrations and APIs
Copy, images, internal links, metadataBooking, payment or e-commerce systems
Search, assistant and conversion improvementsMember logins, portals or gated areas
Technical health, uptime, broken links, speedMigration to a different platform or host

The test is simple. If it is work on the site as it exists, it is inside the retainer. If it makes the site do something it could not do before, it is a project. Where a request sits on the line, it is priced before anything starts, never after.

Quoted as its own project

  • The new website build, design, development, migration and launch
  • Anything in the right hand column above
  • Brand identity work
  • Video production
  • Professional photography and the photographer's fee
  • Any second site, microsite or booking system

Charged at cost, approved first

  • Advertising spend on any platform
  • Mailchimp, hosting, domains and software subscriptions
  • Stock imagery and licensed assets
  • Travel and attendance at events or site visits

Beyond the scope

Work outside the six functions, or beyond the capacity this retainer reserves, is quoted as a fixed price before it starts. Nothing is begun on that basis without written approval, and nothing is ever invoiced as a surprise.

Not the role

Answering venue enquiries, quoting clients, negotiating with venues, managing bookings or any part of the venue finding service itself. The role brings the enquiry to CVBS. CVBS converts it.

On the website build

The build is a defined project with its own scope, milestones and fee, quoted separately. It is not absorbed into this retainer. Once it launches, ownership of the live site moves inside the retainer at no additional cost.

6Client obligations

What CVBS supplies, and by when.

Most digital retainers underperform for one reason. The agency is waiting on the client and nobody wrote it down.

  • 1Karen's judgement, monthlyThirty minutes on the phone or a voice note. What a room is actually like, what worked, what she would not do again. This is the raw material for LinkedIn, the city notes and the venue features. Nothing replaces it and nothing can be invented in its place.
  • 2Real photographyVenue and event images CVBS has the right to use. Stock cannot carry a venue finding business. Required before the site launch, then topped up from site visits.
  • 3Approvals within three business daysContent, offers, EDMs and posts. After three business days the item is treated as approved and published so the schedule holds.
  • 4Enquiry outcome data, quarterlyWhat happened to the enquiries: quoted, converted, value. Without it the quarterly review measures traffic instead of revenue, and the retainer can never be judged on the thing that matters.
  • 5Commercial facts, confirmed onceThe commission rate as it is actually charged, the correct team titles, and the decision on what venue rate information may be published. Every one of these appears in public copy and none can be guessed.
  • 6AccessOwner level access to the domain, hosting, analytics, Search Console, Mailchimp, Act! and all social accounts, held in CVBS's name. CVBS owns its own accounts, always.
The trade

Everything on this list is small. Together they are the difference between a channel that produces enquiries and a channel that produces reports. Where an obligation is not met, the affected deliverable moves and is noted in that month's report rather than absorbed silently.

7The fee

Three levels. One monthly figure.

Priced on what the function covers, not on time spent. There is no timesheet, and none is invoiced against.

Head of DigitalRecommended Everything above, plus the strategy owned rather than executed. Two emails a month. One venue or destination feature a month. Four new index cities a year. The monthly assistant check. Quarterly enquiry review. The annual digital plan. $8,650
Head of Digital, full Everything above, plus capacity to open a new channel or a new market each year without something else stopping. Index expansion at two cities a quarter. Campaign work rather than always-on content alone. $10,400

The difference between the levels is not effort, it is reach. At the first level every function is covered but the ones that compound run thin: email drops to a single send and content production becomes reactive rather than planned. The recommended level delivers everything set out in Parts 3 and 4 as written. The third buys room to grow into something CVBS is not doing yet.

Recommended start

The workload is not flat across the year, so the commitment should not be either.

While the site is being built

$6,900 a month plus GST. The site is a project during this period, not yet an asset to be run, so the retainer does not need to carry it. The build is invoiced separately against its own milestones.

From the day the site launches

$8,650 a month plus GST. The step up is triggered by the launch, which is a date, not a performance review. The work genuinely increases the moment there is a fifty page site to own.

What this replaces, and what it adds

The current arrangement buys social content, and social content alone. Five of the six functions set out in Part 2 have nobody doing them today. This is not the same service at a higher price. It is one function becoming six, with one person accountable for all of them.

How it compares

Published Melbourne salary benchmarks put a digital channel manager around $140,000 base. Add superannuation, leave, public holidays, tools, insurance and the weeks nobody is paying for, and the contractor equivalent of that role is roughly $190,000 to $210,000 a year full time. At two and a half days a week that is $95,000 to $105,000. The recommended level sits inside that band, and CVBS carries none of the employment cost.

All fees exclude GST. The Service Edit invoices under its own ABN and is not exclusive to CVBS. This is a contract for services, not employment, and no superannuation, leave or payroll tax attaches to it. Both parties should confirm that treatment with their own accountants before signing, because the test depends on how the arrangement actually operates, not on what the document calls it.

8Terms

The commercial detail.

Short, because everything that matters has already been said.

  • 1InvoicingMonthly in advance, on the first business day, payable within fourteen days. Overdue amounts pause the work rather than accrue interest, and the pause is notified in writing first.
  • 2CapacityThe engagement reserves two and a half days a week for CVBS. That capacity is a commitment held open, not a timesheet drawn down. No hours are counted, kept or invoiced against, and quiet weeks are not credited any more than heavy ones are surcharged. What is delivered is what is set out in Parts 3 and 4.
  • 3Term and noticeAn initial term of six months, continuing monthly after that. Sixty days written notice from either side. Work in progress at the end of the notice period is completed and handed over.
  • 4Annual reviewThe fee is reviewed each July with at least CPI applied. A change to the scope is discussed as a change of level first, and as a change of fee second.
  • 5Authority and decisionsDigital activity is recommended and then implemented without needing approval line by line. Four things are always the Directors' decision: money committed on CVBS's behalf, the commission and pricing position, anything published in Karen's or Anthony's name, and any change to the brand. A senior title without this clause is a title only.
  • 6OwnershipCopy, images, designs and page content produced for CVBS become CVBS's property once the invoice covering them is paid. The underlying design system, templates, tooling and working files remain The Service Edit's, licensed to CVBS in perpetuity for its own use. CVBS owns every account and domain in its own name from day one.
  • 7HandoverOn termination for any reason, CVBS receives the live site files, the content calendar, the image library, the email templates and the assistant check workbook, within fourteen days. No part of the operation is held hostage.
  • 8ConfidentialityVenue rates, client names, commission arrangements and enquiry data are confidential and are not used in any other engagement or shown as work in progress without written permission.

The rule of the report

A rising named rate with a falling accuracy rate is a worse month, not a better one. The report will say so. Reports that only contain good news are not worth reading.
