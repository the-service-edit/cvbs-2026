What the site now needs from you

The website has been rebuilt around one idea: CVBS only puts forward venues it can speak for. Most of that is done. These are the pieces only CVBS can supply.

Why this list exists

The position is now built. The evidence has to come from you.

Anything that could be written, designed or generated has been. What is left is the part that only exists inside CVBS: who has been where, when, and what they saw. That is the whole point of the position, and it is the one thing a marketplace, a directory or an AI assistant cannot copy.

Nothing on this list has been invented or filled in with a plausible guess. Where a fact was missing, the site prints nothing rather than implying one. That is deliberate, and it is why the page can be trusted. It also means the gaps are visible until you close them.

The two that matter before the meeting

Item 1, the site visit log. It is the position, made checkable. Even ten rows changes what the page is.

Item 3, the two headshots. Four named people is the argument. Two of them currently appear as initials beside two photographs.

The list

Nine things, ranked by what they change

1  The site visit log

A spreadsheet has been left for you at CVBS-site-visit-log-Sydney.csv in the project folder. Fifty three Sydney venues, already filled in with the venue, the room and the precinct. Three columns are blank:

  • Who has been inside (Karen, Anthony, Chantelle or Rychelle)
  • Month and year of that visit
  • What a floor plan would not tell you, in one sentence

Fill in only the rows you are certain of. Leave the rest blank. A blank row prints nothing, so an incomplete log costs you nothing, but a wrong date costs the page its credibility in the same way a wrong capacity would.

What it turns on

The index already carries the field and the filter. The moment one row is filled in, a line appears under that venue reading Walked by Karen, March 2026, a new filter option appears reading Venues we have been inside, and the expanded specification gains a note saying so. Until then the filter option hides itself, because an empty filter is worse than no filter.

2  Confirm and extend the worked-with list

Twelve of the fifty three Sydney venues currently carry a Worked with badge. That is a claim about CVBS's booking history and it was set conservatively. Column four of the same spreadsheet is where you correct it. Add the ones that are missing, and take off any that should not be there.

3  Two headshots and two job titles

Chantelle and Rychelle appear as initials beside photographs of Karen and Anthony, on both the homepage and the about page. Their titles are also a placeholder. Two phone photographs against a plain wall, in the same light, would do it.

4  Two or three more named testimonials

The results page previously carried two quotes attributed to "Sarah M." and "David R.", illustrated with photographs of Karen and Anthony. Those have been removed. The page now carries the two real ones, from Sherida Bolling at Randstad and Angela Moffett at Energex, under a heading that says CVBS does not publish anonymous testimonials.

That heading is a strong claim and it is worth keeping. It needs two or three more real quotes behind it, with the person's name, their organisation, and their permission to publish.

5  Approve two promises now on the site

Two sentences have been added that commit CVBS to something. They are the right commitments, and they are the reason the position is worth having, but they are yours to accept or strike out.

The promiseWhere it appears
We only put forward what we can speak for. If we cannot tell you what a room is actually like to run an event in, it does not belong on your shortlist.About page, and as the lead card on all sixteen destination pages.
You will hear the drawbacks too. A difficult load-in, a low ceiling, a pre-function space that struggles once the room is full. You will hear it from us at shortlist, not on the day.About page, and as the second card on all sixteen destination pages.

The second one is the sharper of the two. It is also the one no competitor makes, which is exactly why it is worth making. It does mean saying something unflattering about venues that pay you commission, so it is a decision rather than a piece of copy.

6  Photography for nine destinations

Eight destination pages shared a single generic photograph, including Byron Bay, the Yarra Valley, the Mornington Peninsula and the Sunshine Coast. Perth had a blank placeholder file. Publishing the wrong photograph of a region you claim to know contradicts the position in the most literal way available, so those nine now use a designed panel built from the precincts you actually source in.

That is honest and it looks deliberate, but real photography would be better. If your team has phone footage or stills from site visits, that is the ideal source, because it is evidence rather than stock.

Also worth replacing: the Shangri-La Sydney image is only 259 pixels wide and looks soft beside the others.

7  The day delegate package decision

Nobody in Australia publishes day delegate package ranges. Capacity data is everywhere, some rates are published, DDP bands are not. It is the single biggest opening available and it is a decision only you can make.

It does not have to be precise. A band, with the date it was last checked, would be the most generous thing published in the category.

8  Confirm the commission rate

The site says a standard commission and the budget calculator implies ten per cent. Confirm the real figure so nothing on the site contradicts what you say in a room.

9  Answer one question, separately, without conferring

What do we want CVBS to be known for?

One line each from Karen, Anthony, Chantelle and Rychelle. Written down, not discussed first. Whether the four answers match is the most useful piece of information available about this business right now.
